Instrumented 8K delivery

Prism

Prism is an instrumented platform for producing live 8K broadcasts and delivering them to the screens. It takes a real contribution — a cinema camera or its own instrumented source — encodes it, packages it in the cloud, and delivers it globally, measuring true per-stage latency the whole way. Because every frame carries its own timestamp (stamped in, or filmed off an optical slate), Prism measures all of this without guessing.

The measurement model

One timecode, followed from the lens to the screen

Every frame carries a QR timecode stamped with its emission epoch. Latency at any point is simply that frame's own age when it is seen there — no cross-clock arithmetic, no assumptions. With a real camera there are two references, kept separate on purpose: pipeline latency (T0→T6, from the encoder's stamp) and glass-to-glass (acquisition + pipeline, from the scene itself). The acquisition stage exists because of the camera slate page — a full-screen QR clock (with stamp-hold and sync bands) that any display can show and any camera can film, putting real-world time onto the glass itself.

Freshness

Precise stages catch the exact frame (frame-level age); freshness stages read the newest served frame. Acquisition is deliberately not a T-stage: it is a same-frame differential — a frame that shows the filmed slate AND carries the encoder's stamp yields camera→capture time with the observer's clock cancelled out — and it carries its own error sources (display lag, exposure, slate↔encoder clock agreement), stated in every report. Measured on the real camera days: latency numbers are lens-referenced off the glass, not just encoder-referenced. A warm-up window keeps pipeline spin-up out of the numbers, and every run resets to zero, so results reflect steady state — not the first noisy seconds.

Capabilities

What it does

The whole chain in one platform — origination or real-camera contribution through playback, with the instrumentation an ordinary encoder can't provide.

Originate

Generate the source — or film it

A test pattern, a pre-rendered file, the instrumented QR-timecode source — or a real camera filming the slate page, a full-screen QR clock that puts scene time on the glass. Any resolution up to 8K.

Encode

Hardware or software

NVENC H.264 / HEVC / AV1 or libx264/libx265, with presets, rate control, and split-frame encoding across multiple NVENC engines for 8K. Never falls back to CPU silently.

Deliver

CMAF, SRT, origin, CDN

CMAF (HLS + DASH) to disk, an SRT contribution, ingest into MediaPackage v2 (HLS, LL-HLS, and DASH from one chunk set, a four-rung ladder — bit-exact 8K HEVC, 4K HEVC, 1080p + 720p H.264 — plus up to 4×7.1 audio), and CloudFront in front with signed origin access.

Measure

Per-frame latency

QR-timecode latency across T0→T6 — precise where the exact frame can be caught, freshness where only the newest segment is visible — plus the acquisition differential when a camera films the slate, so glass-to-glass is measured from the scene, never inferred.

Span continents

Distributed probes

Passive probes on separate instances — including CDN-edge boxes on the US East Coast and in Europe — measure real cross-network hops with clock-synced one-way timing.

Operate & observe

Console + monitor + self-check

An always-on seven-view console (fleet control, live ops with egress-surface health and event log, five-step test builder, typed Reports with two-run diffing), the hosted Confidence monitor with per-channel audio meters, and a one-click instrument health check. Every run writes a diffable report; quality is scored per run with VMAF v1 + CAMBI at the one lossy boundary.

What you put it to

What it lets you test

The questions a delivery pipeline actually raises — answered on real hardware and a real network, with numbers you can trust and compare.

Throughput

Real-time headroom

Can this GPU hold 1× (or N×) at a given resolution, frame rate, codec, and preset? Live speed, fps, and drop telemetry answer it on the actual instance — e.g. 8K60 HEVC at 1.0×.

End to end

Glass-to-glass latency

The true source→viewer delay, with a per-stage breakdown — encode vs. SRT vs. packaging vs. delivery vs. player — so you can see which latency dominates.

Across the wire

Cross-machine latency

Measure a genuine SRT contribution between instances, over the real network, with the one-way timing that only synchronized clocks make honest.

Trade-offs

Codec & config A/B

Sweep any mix of codec, preset, resolution, frame rate, segment duration, and bitrate; every report embeds its configuration and diffs directly against another.

Integrity

Timecode survivability

Confirm the QR survives the encode at a given bitrate and resolution before trusting any number — a pass/fail gate, not a guess.

Integrity

Known-delay calibration

Inject a measured delay at one stage and confirm that only that stage moves — the correlation check that makes the whole chain believable.

The rig

Hardware & cloud

The validated results run on AWS EC2 — a small fleet spanning three regions — but nothing here is AWS-specific.

Encoder

NVIDIA L40S · g6e

Three NVENC engines drive the split-frame 8K60 encode; runs the headless campaign engine the console relays to.

Mux + packager

NVIDIA L4 · g6

Receives the SRT contribution, packages CMAF / HLS, pushes the two-variant ladder into MediaPackage, and transcodes the 1080p preview — without ever touching the encoder's pace.

Control plane

Console · t3.small

Always on: TLS + per-user auth, the pipeline graph, fleet start/stop, campaign relay, the hosted player, and the instrument health check. Survives fleet power-cycling.

CDN edges

Probe boxes · 2 continents

Small CPU instances in us-east-1 and eu-central-1 decode the QR straight off their local CloudFront edge. They power on and off with the fleet — zero standby cost between runs.

AWS glue

Time Sync · MediaPackage · CloudFront

The Amazon Time Sync Service keeps chrony µs-accurate (what makes cross-machine timing honest); MediaPackage v2 is the managed origin; CloudFront fronts it with signed origin access so the CDN is the only public door when that switch is thrown.

Not AWS-locked

Prism is Python + FFmpeg + NVENC/NVDEC, so it runs on any Linux GPU host. The AWS pieces map straight across: EC2 GPU instances → GCP A3/G2 or Azure NV/NC series; the Time Sync Service → any chrony/PTP/NTP source; security groups → the platform's firewall controls; MediaPackage → any LL-capable origin. Same rig, different cloud.

Where it stands

Validated so far — and what's left

The chain is proven end to end at 8K — from a real camera, through a managed origin, to a real player. What remains extends audio width, low-latency granularity, and resilience — none of it blocks the numbers that already work.

Validated

18 confirmed

Demonstrated on real hardware, with pass/fail gates where it counts.

  • Timecode survivability 600 / 600 frames (100%) decoded through 8K HEVC NVENC — the QR carrier survives the encode.
  • Correlation integrity a known +500 ms delay injected at the encode and SRT stages rose alone (+471 ms and +523 ms respectively), with no other stage affected. The injection gate is fully closed.

  • Per-region CDN delivery CloudFront with signed origin access, measured from three edges: home region ≈3.6 s, US East ≈4.5 s, Frankfurt ≈5.4 s cumulative — the first real geographic propagation numbers.
  • Player closes the chain the Prism Player decodes the QR at playback and reports T6 — every stage T0→T6 measured in one live run.
  • The instrument checks itself a one-click canary asserts every probe reported and every stage measured; its first run caught a real probe defect, and the verdict trail (unhealthy → fix → healthy) is on record in the audit log.
  • Cross-machine clocks µs-synced to the AWS time source, so one-way SRT timing is trustworthy.
  • Multichannel audio rides free 16 channels (2×7.1 AAC) through the full chain with video latency moving ≤~3% at every stage — measured in paired A/B runs on the fleet, not assumed. 32 channels (4×7.1) proven through a real NVENC encode locally, 32/32 per-channel identity intact.
  • Channel identity is checkable live any channel soloed at the source mid-run via a runtime filter command (no restart, container timing untouched), auto-verdicts from the player's per-channel FFT, auditable line-check records saved.
  • Quality scored where pixels change VMAF v1 (4K@1.5H, native 4K windows — no downscaling) + CAMBI banding, frame-exact-aligned by the QR, written into every campaign report.
  • A real camera, live 8K59.94 from a cinema camera over quad-link SDI, live through the whole chain: 10-minute runs at 60 fps / 1.00× / zero drops, 75–82 Mbps delivered, with lens-referenced latency anchored by a filmed optical slate.
  • Open-ended transmission on the real estate rolling window reports, a reconnecting contribution, and honest drop accounting — a live 20 s packager outage was counted as one 26.2 s drop window and the run survived it.
  • Contribution rail at the ceiling streamid-filtered SRT ingest held 8K59.94 HEVC at 140 Mbps with zero drops on worst-case content; between-session refresh is automatic and re-entry takes seconds.
  • Surround at the edge 5.1 through the managed origin with 6/6 channel identity confirmed at the CDN; wire formats are measured per encoder build and unsurvivable layouts are refused before they ship.
  • Fractional-rate exactness at 59.94 the declared and delivered segment durations agree to the microsecond (whole-GOP segment snapping + actual-duration manifests) — cadence drift is structurally gone.
  • Recording & remote contribution streaming capture to object storage with integrity hashes and frame-exact clips; on-prem encoder boxes join the fleet and start/stop their SRT contribution from the console over an outbound-only control plane.

Still to validate

4 open

Width, granularity, and resilience — none blocks the core numbers.

  • 8-channel audio through the managed origin 5.1 is proven at the edge; wider layouts need the direct-origin audio lane (built, dark) or an origin-side fix — the remaining width question, not a correctness one.
  • Sub-segment low latency the LL-HLS endpoint publishes real partial segments, but the probes read whole-segment freshness — part-level probing is what proves the low-latency story downstream of packaging.
  • Encoder redundancy & sync run a hot-standby encoder frame- and timecode-aligned with the primary, and exercise the failover workflow end to end.
  • Camera-clock discipline the filmed-slate acquisition stage is live; a PTP-disciplined camera clock is what tightens the lens-referenced numbers further. Hardware specified.

Planned

More formats and modes for the pipelines you actually run.

Reach & production

Exploratory

The larger platform Prism is growing toward.

  • Hardware PTP clock — the on-prem contributor disciplined by a GPS PTP grandmaster, tightening lens-referenced timing further. Hardware specified and priced; the camera chain itself is now proven (see Validated).
  • Super-resolution, census-first — frontier upscalers measured honestly on the fleet GPU: the first census point puts diffusion-based 4K→8K at ~0.12 fps (~500× off real time) on a single 48 GB card via 2×2 tiling. Realtime remains a 1080p→4K story; venue-scale needs multi-GPU tiling. The curve, not the hype, drives what ships.
  • Venue playout integration — driving the screen media servers directly from the delivery chain, closing the last hop.
  • More edge regions — the two-continent probe fleet is live and one script-run per region to grow (Asia-Pacific, South America).
Honest by design

Every stage is labeled measured, derived, or not measured — Prism never presents an estimate as a measurement. T1/T2 are measured per frame on the instrumented path; T3–T6 are freshness readings taken wherever a real endpoint serves. When the instrument itself was the problem — as its own health check proved once — the numbers were corrected and the old ones retracted. What it can't observe cleanly, it says so.
